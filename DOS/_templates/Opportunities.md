# Opportunities — org, operational & strategic improvement ideas

Ideas for improving the org **beyond your own team**: things worth *raising*, not things you
have agreed to *do*. Ammunition for the rooms where altitude is the currency — your manager,
skip-level, CTO, product lead, product/eng ops, peer managers.

> 🔴 **This file creates no obligations.** An entry sitting untouched for six months is the doc
> working, not rotting. Never surface an entry as a TODO and never let it inflate the action
> list. If you decide to act, it graduates to the Standing Brief — and only then.
>
> That contract is why this tends to be the healthiest file in the system, and it's the
> precedent the `## Trip-wires` block copies. Copy to `DOS/Opportunities.md` and delete this
> block.

## Conventions

- **Append-only.** Don't groom away entries that were raised and declined — the fact that it
  was declined, and why, is the valuable part next time.
- **Bar for inclusion — all four must hold.** Deliberately narrow; every org has infinite
  improvable things and an unbounded list is a dead list.
  1. **It's a diagnosis or a thesis, not a task.** If the next step is "I do X", it's a TODO.
     This doc holds things that explain *why a class of problem keeps recurring*.
  2. **It generalises past its instance** — beyond one ticket, one board, one team. If it only
     applies to your team, it belongs in a project page.
  3. **It has a citable form** — a sentence sayable in a room without five minutes of setup.
  4. **Someone else could own it.** These are ideas for *the org*, which means the answer to
     "who does this?" is legitimately "not necessarily me."
- **Structure per entry:** `Observation` (what was seen, with evidence) → `Mechanism` (why it
  happens — the generalisable part) → `Where it's citable` (which room, which person, what ask).
- **Status tags:** `#unraised` · `#raised` (with who + when) · `#landed` · `#declined` (with the
  reason) · `#superseded`
- **Evidence beats theory.** An entry with a specimen attached ("this happened twice, one board
  apart") is worth ten plausible-sounding ideas. Prefer things observed over things reasoned;
  don't manufacture entries to fill the file.
